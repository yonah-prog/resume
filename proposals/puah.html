01

Overview

PUAH's work has evolved into something far broader than its current website communicates. The organization sits at the intersection of halacha and reproductive medicine, serves three distinct audiences — families, rabbanim, and physicians — and has just launched a Digital Resource Library that presents PUAH as a modern, authoritative, forward-thinking institution.

The corporate website hasn't caught up. This project closes that gap: the same organization, the same content and structure people already know, rebuilt with a modern design language, a refreshed brand system, and the standard functionality a website of this stature is expected to have.

The goal in one line: A modernized PUAH website that carries the same visual authority as the Digital Library — familiar in structure, transformed in look, brand, and function.

What we're solving

  • Dated presentation — the current site undersells an organization that leads its field. The rebuild delivers a design that reads as credible, current, and calm.
  • Brand inconsistency — the Digital Library established a modern visual direction. The corporate site will share that language so the two read as one organization, not two.
  • Three audiences, one entry point — families in crisis, rabbanim seeking guidance, and physicians seeking collaboration each need a clear path from the homepage without competing for attention.
  • Function gaps — real, working forms for hotline inquiries, supervision requests, and general contact, routed to the right inbox and structured so staff can act on them.
  • Mobile reality — the majority of visitors arrive on a phone, often urgently. Every page is designed mobile-first.

Approach

This is not a re-imagining of PUAH from scratch. The existing site structure is the starting point — it reflects how PUAH actually works and how its audiences actually search. We keep what's right about it, tighten what's cluttered, and rebuild the surface entirely: new design system, refreshed brand expression, modern build, working functionality.

A visual direction has already been developed and shared as a working concept, so this engagement begins from an agreed aesthetic starting point rather than a blank page.

02

Scope of Work

The engagement runs in four phases. Each phase ends with something concrete for PUAH to review and approve before the next begins.

1
Discovery & Information Architecture
Weeks 1–2

We begin with the site PUAH already has. Every existing page is inventoried, mapped, and assessed — what stays as-is, what gets rewritten, what gets consolidated, and what's missing. The result is an approved page map that governs the rest of the project.

Includes

  • Full audit and inventory of the existing website
  • Audience mapping across the three core audiences — families and couples, rabbanim and community leaders, physicians and medical professionals
  • Site architecture and navigation structure, with a page-by-page map approved before design begins
  • Content plan: which existing copy carries over, which needs editing, which needs writing
  • Platform and hosting recommendation, confirmed with PUAH's team
  • Definition of every form and where each submission needs to land
2
Brand Modernization & Design System
Weeks 2–3

PUAH's brand is refreshed rather than replaced — the equity in the name and mark is preserved, while the way the brand is expressed on screen is brought fully up to date and aligned with the Digital Library.

Includes

  • Refined color system — primary, secondary, and neutral palette suited to a sensitive medical and halachic subject matter
  • Typography system: display and body typefaces, with a full type scale
  • Logo treatment and usage across the site (refinement and application, not a new logo from scratch)
  • Visual language: photography direction, iconography style, spacing, elevation, and motion principles
  • A reusable component library — buttons, cards, callouts, forms, navigation, footers — so future pages stay consistent by default
  • Design system documented and delivered as a reference for PUAH's team and any future vendor
  • Visual alignment with the Digital Library so both properties read as one brand
3
Page Design
Weeks 3–5

Every page in the approved map is designed for desktop and mobile. Design begins with the homepage and the highest-traffic templates, then extends across the site.

Design coverage

  • Homepage — who PUAH is, the three audiences it serves, and clear routes into each
  • Audience landing pages — dedicated entry points for families, rabbanim, and physicians
  • Services & programs — guidance and support, the PUAHline, lab supervision, financial assistance, and the organization's other core services
  • About — PUAH's story, mission and approach, team, medical advisory council, rabbinic endorsements
  • Initiatives — rabbinic leadership, medical thinktank, education and awareness
  • Contact, inquiry, and hotline pages — designed around how PUAH actually receives and triages requests
  • Support / donate — a clean, credible giving path that hands off to PUAH's existing donation platform
  • Digital Library gateway — a prominent, well-designed bridge from the corporate site into the Resource Library
  • Utility pages — 404, privacy, accessibility statement, and any additional standard pages

Scope is sized to roughly match the existing site. The design system is built so additional content pages can be added later using existing templates — by PUAH's team or as a small add-on engagement.

4
Build, QA & Launch
Weeks 5–8

The site is built as a standalone website — its own codebase, its own hosting, independent of the Digital Library — and deployed on infrastructure PUAH owns.

Includes

  • Full front-end development of every designed page, desktop through mobile
  • All forms built, tested, and routed to the correct recipients with email notification
  • SEO foundations: page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, sitemap, schema markup, and redirects from every existing URL so current search rankings are preserved
  • Performance optimization — image handling, load speed, Core Web Vitals
  • Accessibility pass toward WCAG 2.1 AA (contrast, keyboard navigation, screen-reader labeling, focus states)
  • Analytics installation and event tracking on key conversion points
  • Cross-browser and cross-device QA on real devices
  • Domain, DNS, and SSL cutover managed end to end
  • Launch and handover, including all code, accounts, and credentials
The job isn't done until you're happy — full stop.

Revisions are not counted, capped, or billed separately. We go as many rounds as it takes on the brand, the design, and the build until the site reflects exactly what PUAH wants it to be. This is a collaboration, not a transaction.

03

Functionality

"Standard functionality" means everything a modern organizational website is expected to do — built properly, tested, and handed over working.

Forms & inquiries

  • General contact form with routing to the correct department
  • Guidance / "Ask the Rabbi" style inquiry form with the fields staff actually need to respond
  • Lab supervision request form
  • Financial assistance inquiry form
  • Newsletter or mailing-list signup, connected to PUAH's email platform
  • Spam protection on every form, email notification on submission, and a clear confirmation state for the visitor

Site features

  • Responsive navigation with mobile menu, and prominent phone / hotline access on every page
  • Click-to-call and click-to-email throughout — critical for urgent mobile visitors
  • Donation handoff to PUAH's existing giving platform, presented cleanly
  • Team, advisory council, and endorsement listings built out as structured, repeatable page sections
  • Cross-linking into the Digital Resource Library from relevant pages
  • Social and Open Graph metadata so shared links render properly
  • Analytics dashboard access so PUAH can see traffic and inquiry volume

Not included in this scope

  • Rebuilding or restructuring the Digital Resource Library — it stays as-is; this project aligns to it visually and links to it
  • New long-form content writing beyond editing and adapting PUAH's existing copy
  • Custom photography or video production (stock and PUAH-supplied assets are used; direction is provided)
  • New logo design from scratch — the existing mark is refined and applied
  • Custom application development: patient portals, case management, payment processing, or member logins
  • Ongoing marketing, SEO campaigns, or content production after launch

Anything in this list can be quoted separately if PUAH wants it — it's excluded here to keep the scope and the price honest.
